# POLICY.md

Business rules and institutional policies for ClubSync. These govern behavior that must be enforced in code — not UI preferences.

---

## Authentication & Passwords

- Registration requires an official SCC institutional email (`@sccpag.edu.ph`). No other domain is accepted.
- Password complexity: minimum 8 characters, with at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter and one number. A special character is not required. Registration and password change apply the same rule (`Password::defaults()`).
- **Time-gated password changes:** a user may change their password at most once every 7 calendar days. Enforce server-side using the `lastPasswordChanged` timestamp. Display the next available change date on rejection.
- On failed login, say what went wrong so the person can fix it: an address with no ClubSync account is told it is not registered yet (and pointed to Register); a wrong password is told the password is incorrect (and pointed to Forgot Password); a lost connection — the device's, or the server's to the database or Firebase — is reported as a connection problem, never as wrong credentials. Saying an address is not registered discloses no more than registration's email check already does, and sign-in stays limited to 5 attempts a minute.
- Suspended accounts return: "Your account has been suspended. Please contact the Director of Student Affairs for assistance."
- **Advisers sign in on their own page** (`/login/adviser`, the Adviser tab of the sign-in page), because they are employees, not students: adviser number, SCC email and password, or Continue with Google. Only an adviser account gets in there, and the adviser number must be the account's own; both are checked only after the password is right, so the page reveals nobody's role. The student sign-in still opens every account.
- Session tokens expire after 30 minutes of inactivity. "Remember Me" stores the refresh token in an HttpOnly cookie with a 30-day expiry.
- **Google sign-in is an added method, for the SCC Google Workspace only.** "Continue with Google" sits beside email and password and never replaces them. Only accounts of the `sccpag.edu.ph` Google Workspace are accepted:
  - The server decides from Google's own verified ID token: its hosted-domain (`hd`) claim must be `sccpag.edu.ph`, the address must end in `@sccpag.edu.ph` and be verified, and the token must have been issued to ClubSync for this sign-in (client, issuer, expiry, nonce, state, PKCE). The address alone is never enough, because a personal Google account can be opened with any address. The `hd` hint sent to Google only narrows the account chooser and is not relied on. The domain is fixed in code, not configuration.
  - The check happens **before** Firebase is contacted, so a Google account outside the school never has a Firebase account created for it.
  - A Google account opens only the ClubSync account with the same address. For an account that has a password, the first Google sign-in asks for that password once, and the Google account is then linked to the same Firebase account. Firebase does this on Google's word only for gmail.com. A Google sign-in that reaches a different Firebase account from the one on record is refused and logged, never re-pointed.
  - **Registering with Google sets no password.** An SCC Google account with no ClubSync account is taken to its own registration form. The name and address come from Google and cannot be changed there. The student enters only their EDP number, year level and mobile number, accepts the Terms, then picks a department and course and proves enrollment like everyone else. The account signs in with Google only: its password field is empty, the profile has no password to change, and a password sign-in attempt is told to use Google. If it later gets a password through Forgot Password, the first password sign-in records it, and both methods then work.
  - An address that already has an unfinished registration with a password is sent to the password form to finish it, because Firebase releases that account only to its password.
  - Suspended accounts are refused before Firebase is asked. Every Google sign-in and every link is audit-logged.

## Registration & Academic Club Enrollment

- **The email address is settled before the wizard moves on.** It is checked against both stores at the email field — the `users` table and Firebase Authentication — and registration does not proceed to department or course selection until it is clear. A taken address is reported against the email field itself, never as a failure at the end of the flow.
- Firebase will not answer "is this address taken?" while email enumeration protection is on, so the Firebase account is **created at the email step**, and its refusal to create a duplicate is the check.
- An address held in Firebase with no `users` row is an unfinished registration. If the password entered opens that account it is the same person returning, and they resume and complete it; otherwise the address belongs to someone else and they are told to sign in or reset their password.
- **The student always knows where they are.** Every registration step shows "Step N of 4" (details, department, course, proof of enrollment), and arriving at a step shows a notice saying what it asks for. The notice at step 4 says the account is created only after it.
- **Department and course are read back before anything follows from them.** Continuing from the course step first shows the department, the course and the academic club that course leads to, and asks the student to confirm. Step 4 lists them again with a Change link for each. Until the account is created either may be changed freely, and picking a different department drops the course chosen under the old one.
- **An unfinished registration is resumed by signing in.** Progress is saved after each step so that closing the browser or signing out does not lose it. Signing in with the password that opens the Firebase account (or with Google, for a Google registration) returns the student to the first step not yet answered, usually the proof of enrollment, instead of saying the address is not registered.
  - Only the wizard's own answers are saved: never the password, never a Firebase token. They are encrypted, the address is kept only as an HMAC for lookup, and the saved progress is deleted when the account is created, or after 14 days (`retention.registration_draft_days`, pruned by `retention:check`).
  - A registration started before progress was saved can only be resumed from step 1. Every resume is audit-logged.
- **A student held at proof of enrollment may correct their course.** While the account is held (sent back, or pending with no document waiting for a person), the student can change their department and course from the verification page before uploading again. Nothing rests on the course yet, because academic club membership comes with verification. The change is audit-logged (from and to). It is not allowed while the DSA or an administrator is checking a document, because they are judging it against the course on record.
- **A new student proves they are enrolled before the account opens.** The last registration step asks for a Saint Columban College Certificate of Enrollment (PDF or photo) or the front of the SCC school ID. Nothing else is accepted, and the document must show the same full name, EDP number and course the student registered with.
  - The document is read automatically (Gemini) for its type and the three printed fields; the comparison with the registration is done in code, and the model is never shown what the student entered. A clean match verifies the account at once.
  - A clear mismatch — wrong kind of document, the back of the ID, another school, or a name / EDP number / course that differs — is refused with the reason, and no account is created. After two refusals the student may send the document to a person instead.
  - When the automatic check cannot decide (Gemini unavailable, or the document looks edited) or the student asks for a person, the account is created but held on the verification page until the **DSA** — or an **administrator** — approves it. Rejecting requires a reason, which the student sees, and they may upload another document.
  - **A document held only because Gemini was unavailable is saved as Pending Verification and re-checked automatically once Gemini is back**: every 15 minutes (`ai:catch-up`), or at once when the DSA or an administrator presses "Check them now". The re-check settles it as the check would have at upload. A match verifies the account and admits the student. A clear mismatch sends it back to the student with the reasons, which name the field that did not match but never repeat the value read. A document that looks edited stays with the DSA, who is told. A decision a person made first always stands. Documents flagged as edited, and those the student chose to hand to a person, are never re-checked. Students and the DSA are told the case will be re-checked, so nobody has to clear an outage's backlog by hand.
  - Academic club enrollment (below) happens when the account is verified, not before.
  - RA 10173: a document that passes automatically is never stored. One waiting for a person is stored encrypted and deleted the moment it is decided; only the outcome (document type, method, reviewer, time) is kept. Every check and decision is audit-logged without the values read.
  - Students who registered before this rule, and staff, are not asked to verify.
- **Advisers sign up on their own form** (`/register/adviser`): adviser number, full name, SCC email and password — or their SCC Google account in place of the email and password. There is no department, course or proof of enrollment, and the account opens at once with the `adviser` role. The adviser picks the club they advise from the active clubs with no adviser; that is a **request**, and an administrator confirms or declines it on Manage Users before the adviser is linked to the club, so no one who signs up can take a club alone. While every club has an adviser they sign up with no club, and an administrator gives them one when a club is free. The administrators are notified of every adviser sign-up.
- **Academic club membership is automatic and needs no approval.** When registration is complete — the proof of enrollment is verified, automatically or by the DSA or an administrator — the student becomes an **active member** of the academic club(s) mapped to their course at once. Officers do not accept them and the adviser does not approve them, because the verified enrollment already shows they belong. The student is welcomed in-app, and the club's officers and adviser are told for their records, with nothing to act on.
  - The course→academic-club mapping is the trigger; the student does not search for or join their academic club by hand.
  - A Join request the student had already made to that club becomes the membership. One the adviser had already turned down is left as decided.
- **Every other membership still needs approval:** a student who presses Join on a non-academic club, or on an academic club that is not their course's, goes through the two steps. The **club officers accept** the applicant, then the **club adviser gives final approval**. The Director of Student Affairs does not approve club membership. Until approved, an applicant may see the club exists but cannot access its content (activities, announcements).
- **A signed-in student with no course on record is held at the course step.** Without a course they belong to no academic club and never will, so the system requires the selection before any other page is reachable, then enrols them exactly as registration would. Advisers, the DSA and administrators are exempt — they have no course by nature.
- If no club is mapped to a course, complete registration, flag the student's profile as `Pending Club Assignment`, and alert the DSA Admin. Inform the student their club assignment is pending review. The flag is re-evaluated whenever the student's course is set, so mapping the course later clears it without anyone doing so by hand.

## Role-Based Access & Churn Risk

- **Churn Risk data (engagement scores and risk tags) must never be visible to Student Members** — not even in aggregate or anonymized form. Enforce at both the UI and the server route/middleware level; return 403 on direct URL access.
- Churn risk is scoped to Club Officers, who see only their own club's members.
- Club Advisers and the DSA do not have access to the Churn Risk Engine.
- **The DSA follows clubs, not members.** In place of churn risk, the DSA sees how well each club has been doing over the semester — activities held and their turnout month by month, presence updates, fee collection, semester reports and unresolved compliance notices, combined into an explained club score. These are club-level figures only: no member is scored, tagged or named there.
- An officer who belongs to multiple clubs gets elevated privileges per-club, not globally.
- **Officer positions:** President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Auditor, Media Mass Officer (Internal) and Media Mass Officer (External). The old single MMO post stays valid for whoever holds it but is no longer given.
- **An administrator gives an officer position in a named club.** On Manage Users, choosing a position asks which club, among the clubs the student is an active member of; a student in PSITS, BYTE and Debate made Secretary is Secretary of the one club picked. A student holds one post at a time, so a new post replaces the old one wherever it was; a club has one holder of each post, so whoever held it becomes a Member of the club (and of no other club, an officer returns to the Member role). The confirmation names who is replaced. A role change takes effect the next time that person signs in.
- **Adviser accounts are for employees only.** A student account (one with an EDP number or a course) is never made an adviser, and an adviser account is never made a member or officer. Advisers create their own account (§Registration).
- **One adviser per club, one club per adviser.** A club with an adviser is never given a second one — on Manage Users or on the DSA's club form; the adviser must be taken off first. Moving an adviser to another club leaves their old club with none, and an adviser who stops being an adviser leaves their club with none.
- **Officers serve for a school year.** The summer semester closes a school year and the next one begins, and every club appoints a new set of officers for it. A club that has appointed nobody since the school year began is asked to — its adviser is notified once per year and the DSA can see which clubs have not answered.
- **Turnover never revokes access on a date.** The sitting officers keep working until the club actually replaces them, so no club is left unable to run itself because a term rolled over. Who holds a post is changed by the adviser appointing someone, not by the calendar.

## Announcements & Content Workflow

- Club officers publish their own club's announcements and letters directly. There is no approval queue in front of an announcement, and no role reviews one before it reaches members.
- An announcement is `Published` the moment the officer posts it. Only its author may edit it afterwards, and an edit does not re-notify members.
- Where an announcement was AI-drafted, the officer reading and posting it **is** the human gate §AI & Automation requires: the draft is returned to the officer's form and nothing is published until they press Publish.
- Adviser review applies to **activities**, not announcements — see §Event Creation.

## Event Creation

- **Every activity an officer adds** — internal meetings, ACLE, community involvement, campus resource use and other external activities alike — needs **two approvals, in this order: the club Adviser, then the Vice President for Administration.** Nothing is scheduled outright; an activity is confirmed only when both have approved. (Internal meetings scheduled before this rule keep their `no_approval_needed` status until they are next changed.) They are sent with a **room booking letter** laid out as `docs/BOOKING OF ROOMS.docx` (the SCC crest and the club logo; the letter to the VPA; the officer's signature, "Noted by" the adviser, "Approved by" the VPA).
  1. **The officer** fills in the activity and the letter's body. The body is drafted by the AI from the activity's details, or written from a template when the AI is unavailable; the officer reads and edits it on the form. Pressing Send with no letter drafts one and stops so it is read first. The letter must name the room being booked. Sending signs it with the officer's name and position (e.g. "DES Club President") and saves the activity as `pending_approval`; the adviser is notified.
  2. **The club adviser** approves the letter, which signs it under "Noted by" and moves the activity to `pending_vpa` (the officers and the VPA are notified), or rejects it with remarks. An adviser may withdraw an approval they gave, also while the letter is with the VPA.
  3. **The Vice President for Administration** — a `vpa` account, run by the VPA or by the SSG for them — sees a letter only once the adviser has approved it. The VPA approves it, which signs it under "Approved by" and confirms the activity — the moment it becomes real to members: they are notified and it is copied onto their calendars — or returns it with remarks (a reason is required), which makes it `rejected` and releases the room. The VPA decides for every club but sees only what a booking letter carries.
  - Until 2026-10-19 the order was the VPA first, then the adviser. Letters the VPA had not yet read then went to the adviser instead; a letter the VPA had already approved is confirmed by the adviser's approval.
- Each signature is recorded at the moment it is given, with the signature image its owner had on file then, so a letter never changes afterwards. Signatures are optional: officers, advisers and the VPA may draw or upload one on their profile; a letter signed without one says it was signed electronically. Signatures are kept privately and appear only on letters their owner signed. The letter downloads as a PDF for the club's officers and adviser, the VPA and the DSA.
- An adviser decides only for their own club. The DSA retains oversight — monitoring, filtering and reporting on every club's activities — but does not approve or reject them.
- If no active Adviser is assigned to a club, the activity still waits in `pending_approval` and the DSA Admin is alerted; if there is no VPA account, it waits in `pending_vpa` and the DSA is alerted — so nothing is left waiting on nobody. Activities sent before the VPA step existed stay with the adviser alone.
- Every letter sent, AI letter drafted, adviser decision and VPA decision is audit-logged.
- Required fields: Event/Activity Title, Date, Venue (from predefined SCC venue list), Purpose/Objectives, Expected Number of Participants. Block submission if any are missing.
- Venues must come from the SCC-configured list — not free-text. Only rooms on the main campus can be booked: AVR; EB 2-1 to 2-5, EB 3-1 to 3-5 and EB 4-5; Moot Court; JD 1 and JD 2; PC 1-1 to 1-4, PC 2-1 to 2-6 and PC 3-1; PC Lab A, B and C; SN 1-1 to 1-3, SN 2-2, SN 2-3, SN 3-1 and SN 3-2; ST 1-1 and ST 1-2; Library; YDC.
- A room is exclusive: a booking is refused when the same room is already held for an overlapping time on that date (back-to-back bookings are allowed). Cancelled and rejected activities release the room. An activity booked before this room list took effect keeps its original venue when edited.
- Officers may edit any activity at any time, including ones that have already taken place. A change to any activity — approved, with the adviser, returned, or a meeting scheduled before every activity needed approval — signs the letter again in the editing officer's name and sends it back to the adviser (`pending_approval`), clearing the adviser's and VPA's signatures; this is also how a returned letter is sent again. A change to the title, date, time or room is refused while the letter still reads as it did, so the officer redrafts or edits it first. Saving with nothing changed leaves the approvals as they are.
- A new activity must start in the future. The date alone is not enough: an activity dated today but starting at a time that has already passed is refused, and the officer is told to pick a later time or a future date. This applies to creating an activity, not to editing one that has already happened.

## Club Fees

- A fee is billed to the club's active members when it is created; each of them is notified, and the fee appears under Fees on their My Activity page.
- An officer may **postpone** a fee to a later due date, or **cancel** it, with an optional reason that members see. The members who still owe a postponed fee are notified of the new date. Everyone billed for a cancelled fee is notified: those who had already paid are told to ask the treasurer about a refund.
- Cancelling is final. A cancelled fee is owed by no one, cannot be edited or paid, and leaves amounts due, calendars, club performance and narrative reports. Payments already recorded stay on record with their official receipts.
- A member is notified when their own payment is recorded or undone, and when a fee's amount or due date is edited.

## AI & Automation

- **No AI-generated content may be dispatched automatically.** All AI outputs (compliance notifications, post-event narratives) must pass through a human review queue before being sent or published. This gate cannot be bypassed by any role.
- If the Gemini API is unavailable: disable AI-assisted features gracefully, keep manual alternatives fully functional, and flag AI-generated drafts as "AI Unavailable — Manual Draft Required."
- **The semester narrative report is the five DSA documents**, laid out as the templates in `docs/resources-dsa` (the SCC banner on every page; the Grand Student Council, SCC and club logos on the cover): the Accomplishment & Documentation Report, Minutes of Meetings, Financial Transactions & Liquidation Report, Property Inventory Report, and Communication Letters & Publication Materials. They are drafted at the end of each semester, or when the DSA concludes it. The AI writes only the prose — each activity's introduction, rationale, minutes (meetings only) and summary recommendations — from the activity records and what the officers attached; tables, attendance lists, figures and photos are compiled from the records, never generated. The adviser reviews and edits every section before submitting, and the documents are handed over as editable Word files that also download as PDFs.
- What a submitted report printed stays as it was: the activities it covers can no longer gain or lose report photos or change their budget, and photos added after submission are left out of it.
- **The DSA may return a submitted report for revision**, with remarks saying what to change (they are required). It goes back to the club adviser as a draft, and the adviser and the club's officers are notified with the remarks. While it is back, the adviser edits it as before and the officers can attach photos and records to its activities again. When the adviser submits it again the documents are frozen afresh, replacing the ones the DSA had, and the DSA is told it was revised. A club with no adviser cannot have its report returned until one is assigned. Returns and resubmissions are audit-logged.
- While a report is a draft, the adviser may have the AI **redraft one activity's sections**: one it could not draft, or one to be rewritten. The new draft replaces only that activity's sections and is reviewed like the first. If the AI cannot answer, nothing changes and the adviser writes the sections.
- A temporary Gemini failure (rate limiting, or a 500/502/503/504 answer) is retried twice, a few seconds apart, before an AI feature gives way to its manual path. A lost connection is not retried, so offline use falls back at once.
- **An outage is remembered for two minutes.** When Gemini cannot be reached, does not answer in time, or still answers 429/502/503/504 after the retries, every AI feature takes its manual path immediately instead of each waiting out its own timeout, and AI buttons say the AI is unavailable instead of offering a draft that would fail. The first request after the two minutes checks whether Gemini is back. A 500 or a 400 does not count as an outage, because Google returns those for a single request it cannot handle, and one awkward request must not switch the AI off for everyone.
- **Every AI feature has a manual path that needs no AI:**
  - Announcements and letters: **templates** for the common cases (meeting, invitation, fee reminder, schedule change, call for volunteers, thank-you, venue request, speaker invitation). They are always offered, including offline and with no AI configured. They fill in only the structure and leave every detail in [brackets], and the page warns before a bracket is posted.
  - Activity proposals: the form and the venue availability check work without AI. The AI only drafts description, purpose, turnout and year levels.
  - Room booking letters: the template writes the body from the activity's own details (title, date, time, room, purpose, expected turnout), flagged "AI Unavailable — Manual Draft Required". An AI draft that adds its own greeting or closing has those lines removed, and one that does not name the room is discarded for the template.
  - Proof of enrollment: Pending Verification and the automatic re-check (§Registration).
  - Semester narrative report: **an activity the AI could not draft starts from a template written from its records**. It contains the details as entered, the recorded attendance, what the officers attached and rule-based recommendations. No prose is generated and no figure is calculated, so it passes the same review checks as an AI draft. It is flagged "AI Unavailable — Manual Draft Required" and marked as a template draft. The adviser may also fill any activity in from its records. Once Gemini is back, `ai:catch-up` has the AI draft every template activity the adviser left unchanged, and tells the adviser which. An activity the adviser edited is theirs and is never replaced. A submitted report is never touched.
  - Compliance statements: the DSA writes the statement.
- The Gemini API key travels in a request header, never in a URL, so it cannot leak into logs through an error message.

## Data Privacy (RA 10173)

- Collect only the minimum PII necessary (name, institutional email, EDP number, course, financial records).
- All PII must be encrypted at rest.
- Cross-club data access is blocked at middleware level — users cannot access data from clubs they are not members or officers of.
- Every data modification, approval action, login attempt, and AI content generation event must be written to the tamper-resistant audit log with timestamp, actor UID, and affected resource.
- Audit logs are retained for a minimum of one complete academic year.
- **Report photos** (activity photos, minutes, programs, letters, publication materials, receipts, properties) are attached only by the club's own officers, kept in the private reporting folder, and served only to those officers and, inside the report documents, to the club adviser and the DSA. Every upload is re-encoded, which strips camera metadata including GPS location. Attachments and removals are audit-logged. The attendance annex lists attendees by name only.

## Private Messaging (Chat)

Any active user may message any other active user one-to-one. Because conversations are personal data under RA 10173, these rules apply on top of §Data Privacy:

- **Participants only.** A conversation can be read or written only by its two participants. No role can open another person's conversation, including DSA, advisers, officers and administrators. A non-participant gets 404 (not 403), so the conversation's existence is not disclosed either.
- **Notice before use.** Chat stays closed until the user acknowledges the in-app privacy notice. The notice says what is collected, why, who can read it, how long it is kept, and the user's rights. The acknowledgement date is stored and audit-logged. If chat's data handling changes, the notice text must change with it.
- **Encrypted at rest; no plaintext copies.** Message bodies are encrypted in the database. Message text must never be copied into notifications, email, broadcasts (WebSocket payloads carry ids and counts only), application logs, the audit log, reports, the Churn Risk Engine or any AI prompt.
- **Minimal directory.** People search requires at least 2 characters, returns at most 10 matches, and discloses only name, a coarse role label (Student / Club Officer / Club Adviser / Student Affairs Office / Office of the VP for Administration) and profile photo. It never discloses email, EDP number, course, year level, officer position or admin status. Suspended users are not listed and cannot be messaged.
- **Audit metadata, not content.** Sending, unsending, deleting a conversation, blocking/unblocking and acknowledging the notice are audit-logged with actor, affected resource and counterpart. The text itself is never logged.
- **User control.** A sender may unsend any of their own messages at any time; the text is destroyed, not hidden. Deleting a conversation removes it from that user's view only; once both participants have deleted it, the messages are permanently erased. A user may block another, which stops both sides sending.
- **Retention.** Messages are permanently deleted after `CHAT_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 365, floor 30) by the daily `chat:prune` job.
- **No offline copies.** The service worker never caches `/chat` pages or responses, and chat pages opt out of Turbo's snapshot cache, so a conversation is not left readable on a shared computer after logout.
- **Not a notification channel.** New messages raise the unread chat badge and a content-free toast. They do not create entries in the notifications table and are never emailed.

## Notifications

- The in-system notification is the system of record: every notification MUST be persisted in-app so offline users see it on next login. No SMS integrations.
- Institutional email (the SCC Google Workspace / Gmail, `@sccpag.edu.ph`) MAY be used as an **added** delivery channel that mirrors an in-system notification — never as a replacement for it, and never as the only place a notification exists. Email is best-effort: a failed send must not block the in-app notification or the originating action.
- Email copies are limited to institutional recipients and institutional content (e.g. published club announcements). No third-party/personal email domains are used for outbound notifications.
- **No AI-generated content may be emailed automatically.** AI-drafted compliance/violation notices remain in-system only and still require human review before dispatch (see Announcements & Content Workflow); the email channel does not bypass that gate.
- Dismissal state is stored in the database; dismissed notifications must not reappear.
- **A member is told every time an officer records their attendance**: checked in, checked out (with the whole record), or a record removed. When the activity is marked completed its attendance is concluded, and everyone who was checked in is told their final record, including that no check-out was recorded when none was. The notice is written from the record as it stands when it is sent, so a check-in undone a moment later is not announced. An officer recording their own attendance is not notified.

## External Integrations

- Scheduled records MAY be copied into the personal Google Workspace calendar of each user they concern — and only those users, so the copy never widens who can see a club's data:
  - **Confirmed club activities** (approved by the club adviser and the VPA) → the club's active members the activity targets by year level, its officers, and its adviser. Pending applicants do not receive them.
  - **Fee due dates** → only the members who still owe that fee. The copy is removed once the payment is recorded. Individual payment status is never written to a calendar.
  - **Semester start and end dates** → every user.
- Sync runs in the background via queued jobs and a service account with domain-wide delegation. It must never block or roll back the originating action, and a sync failure is logged, not surfaced as a user error.
- The database remains the source of truth; calendar copies are a convenience mirror. When a record stops applying to a user (activity rejected, reset to pending or retargeted; member leaves; fee paid or cancelled; adviser replaced), their copy is removed.
- Only institutional Google Workspace credentials are used, and only `@sccpag.edu.ph` accounts are synced. Suspended users receive no new copies. The service-account key is never committed to the repository.

## Club Registry

- **A school year runs first semester → second semester → summer**, and a semester records which term it is and which school year it belongs to. A school year is named for the calendar year it starts in: August 2026 through the summer that follows is `2026-2027`.
- Exactly one semester is flagged current. Everything scoped to a semester — member presence, narrative reports, officer turnover, the archival guard — reads the school year from it, and fails closed when none is flagged rather than guessing.
- The registry launched with 19 academic clubs (course-mapped) and 23 non-academic clubs (open to all students), for 42 total. The registry is maintained by the Administrator and is the authoritative source for auto-enrollment and the AI chatbot's recommendations.
- **The DSA may permanently delete a club**, confirmed by typing the club's acronym. Everything the club owns goes with it: memberships and applications, officer posts, activities and their attendance, announcements, fees with their payments and receipts, narratives and reports with their files, notifications, and its Firebase and Google Calendar copies. The audit log is the one exception — it keeps the club's history and records the deletion (§Data Privacy retention). Officers of no other club return to the Member role, and students whose course no other academic club covers are flagged `Pending Club Assignment` (§Registration). Marking a club inactive is the non-destructive alternative.
- The four SCC colleges for enrollment routing: College of Teacher Education, Arts and Sciences; College of Business Education (CBE); College of Criminology (COC); College of Computing Studies (CCS).
